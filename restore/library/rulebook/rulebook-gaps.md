# B.A.R.D. Core Rulebook: questions for D

These are the 14 places where the October 2025 Core Rulebook (`BARD Core Rulebook.pdf`) leaves a rule unclear, contradicts itself, or leaves out a number the app needs in order to run a table. Each one says what the book says, what is missing, why it would come up in play, and a possible answer. The suggestions are only suggestions; every call is yours.

The numbers (G1 to G17, with G12, G16 and G17 removed because R has settled them) match the full report, [rulebook-coverage-report.md](rulebook-coverage-report.md), which has the evidence and the older-version comparisons. Page numbers are PDF pages. "Handler" means the game master.

Not on this list: skills, conditions, techniques, spells and gear. Handlers create their own, and you publish the base set, so the book does not need to list every one.

**Who decides:** most questions are yours (D). A few say R, because they can be settled by checking the printed book.

---

### G1. When does a roll count as a fumble?
**Pages 11 and 16. Decide: D.**
The book says a roll of "25% or less of the DC" is a fumble: the check fails and something bad happens. It does not say whether "roll" means the d6 alone or the final total with bonuses added.
It matters because the two readings give very different tables. If it is the total, a DC 5 or lower task can never fumble, and at DC 10 it takes a die of 1 with almost no bonuses. If it is the die alone, a DC 10 task fumbles on a 1 or 2, about a third of the time. The older 2.1 draft used the die alone.
*Possible answer:* use the total, rounded down (the same way "within 2 of the DC" works for partial success), and accept that fumbles are rare on easy tasks.

### G2. What gives a character advantage, and what is disadvantage?
**Pages 10 and 27. Decide: D.**
The book says that if Luck "grants advantage", the player rolls extra d6 equal to their Luck and keeps the best total. It never says what grants advantage. Later, the driver's Evasive Maneuver "imposes disadvantage on incoming attacks", but disadvantage is never explained anywhere.
It matters because the Handler needs to know when to give advantage and what the bad version looks like.
*Possible answer:* the Handler decides when advantage applies. Disadvantage is the mirror image: roll the extra dice and keep the worst total.

### G3. Which dice can explode?
**Page 11. Decide: D.**
A 6 on the main die rolls again and adds on. The book first says this applies to "all rolls using any die", and lists damage rolls. Two paragraphs later it says it applies "only to the original core 1d6" and that the proficiency die never explodes. Those cannot both be true, because a damage die is not the core d6.
The October character sheet says damage dice do explode. Not covered anywhere: the extra Luck dice from advantage, the helper's d6 and the +1d6 from a focus item.
*Possible answer:* the main d6 and damage dice explode; the proficiency die, helper dice, focus-item die and advantage dice do not. That follows the character sheet but goes further than page 11's second sentence.

### G4. How does helping another character work?
**Pages 10 and 29. Decide: D.**
Anyone helping adds a d6 plus their skill bonus, but must first succeed at an easy (Cat-D) check. There is no limit on how many people can help. The ritual rules (page 29) use a different system: each helper rolls against DC 10 and adds just +1.
It matters because a big group could pile up helpers and trivialize a hard roll.
*Possible answer:* keep both rules as written and cap the number of helpers on ordinary checks (for example three).

### G5. Sanity loss: exact amounts, the roll to resist, and disorders
**Pages 16 and 17. Decide: D.**
The sanity loss table gives ranges ("2-3 points", "3-5", "8+"), and magic gives ranges too (page 30), but the app needs one amount per event. The book does not say how to pick within a range. It also does not give a difficulty for the roll to resist trauma, does not say how to round "half loss", and does not say whether the half-loss rule also applies to the sanity damage from anomalous attacks (page 27). On a fumble the loss is "full plus 1 extra point and immediate complications", which needs a clear rule too.
For disorders, the book says to check WIL DC 10 when sanity drops low, then shows a table numbered 1 to 6, but never says that a failed check means you roll a d6 on that table. The older 1.3.1 book had that sentence.
*Possible answer:* each range is rolled within its limits (the Handler can always override); each trauma type gets a default difficulty by category; half rounds up; a partial success on anomalous damage costs half; a failed disorder check rolls a d6 on the table.

### G6. Casting magic: which attribute, and when sanity is lost
**Pages 29 and 30. Decide: D (attribute check with R).**
The book gives three different attributes for the casting roll: Willpower or Intelligence in one place, Wisdom or Intelligence in another (and again in the casting steps), and Willpower in the skill prerequisites. Also unclear: whether the sanity check happens after every cast or only after a success (the casting steps say to make it every time; the older book said after success), who pays sanity in a group ritual, and which category a focus item uses for its "+2 sanity cost per category above D". Finally, page 29 says you need proficiency in an arcane skill to cast, but never says whether the Handler can waive that.
*Possible answer:* "cast magic" uses Willpower unless the player names a skill with its own attribute; a sanity check on every cast; group casters each pay half, rounded up; the focus item counts the spell's category; a missing proficiency is a warning the Handler can override.

### G7. Dying, and what a critical injury does
**Page 15. Decide: D.**
At 0 Health a character is incapacitated and suffers a critical injury, but the book never says how a character dies. The injury check is "usually END against DC 9 or higher", but DC 9 is not one of the difficulty levels on page 9 (7 and 10 are the closest). And the book gives no list of what injuries actually do, leaving it to the Handler. The older 1.3.1 book had a death rule (extra damage builds up; more than END means death) and a six-entry injury table.
It matters most here: this is the rule players ask about the first time someone is shot.
*Possible answer:* bring back the 1.3.1 death rule, and offer the six-entry table as optional base content that Handlers can replace. Use a real difficulty level for the check.

### G8. Armor and damage reduction
**Pages 13, 21 and 27. Decide: D.**
Damage reduction is "END + modifier", a fixed number, and the character sheet agrees. Armor "stats are largely narrative", so there are no armor values. The older books had you roll 1d6 + END + armor each time you were hit, and listed light armor as 1-2, medium as 3-4 and heavy as 5+.
It matters because every character wears something and the app has nothing to put in the box.
*Possible answer:* keep damage reduction a fixed number, and give armor categories flat values using the older size tiers (light 1-2, medium 3-4, heavy 5+).

### G9. Strength in melee, and what "MIN" means
**Pages 21 and 25 to 27. Decide: D.**
The weapon table gives dice and a "MIN" number per category but the book never explains MIN. It also never says whether Strength adds to melee damage (the oldest draft said yes).
*Possible answer:* MIN is the lowest damage a hit can do (which is how the character sheet reads it), and Strength does not add to damage.

### G10. What a character can do on a turn
**Pages 25 and 26. Decide: D.**
The book names Major, Minor and Reaction actions but says only that a turn is "a combination of actions". It never says how many. "Aim" is required for shots beyond 500 feet but is not described anywhere (the 1.3.1 book made it a Minor action for +1). The reaction rules are also unclear on whether Dodge can be used against melee attacks: the heading says "Ranged or Melee", the text says ranged.
*Possible answer:* one Major and one Minor action per turn (Dash already implies this); Aim is a Minor action for +1; Dodge works against any attack and Parry only against melee.

### G11. Numbers for enemy types
**Pages 35 and 36. Decide: D.**
The threat tables give an enemy's Health, damage reduction, damage and sanity cost. They do not give how hard it is to hit, how good its attacks are, or how fast it acts. Human skills are written as notes ("2 Skills +3", "UNIQUE") rather than numbers.
It matters because the Handler needs these to run any fight with a class-based enemy.
*Possible answer:* add a defense number and an attack bonus for each class, taken from the skill bonus the class already lists.

### G13. Difficulty names that do not match the difficulty table
**Pages 10, 26 and 27. Decide: D.**
The table on page 9 runs Cat-F 3, E 5, D 7, C 10, B 13, A 17, S 21, Ω 25. But the book also says "Tough (DC 9)", "Moderate", "Advanced" and "Extreme DC 21", mostly in the vehicle rules. These are leftovers from an older scale, and DC 9 is not on the current table. Page 27's passenger shooting penalties ("DC+2, +4, +7") are labeled with the old names too, which read as fixed numbers.
*Possible answer:* drop the old names and restate each use as a real level (for example, the crash test and Withdraw become Cat-D or Cat-C).

### G14. Advancement purchases
**Page 31. Decide: D.**
"Single use" is listed for Tough Hide, Formidable and Unshakeable (50 XP each). It is not said whether that means once per character or once per purchase. "Request Artifact Clearance (Limited)" has no explanation of what it gives.
*Possible answer:* once per character; Artifact Clearance is a note the Handler tracks.

### G15. The skill list
**Pages 19 and 29. Decide: D.**
Page 29 names Mind Shielding as a skill that lets you cast, but it is not in the skill list on page 19. The agent dossier form lists a default attribute for 43 of the book's 45 skills, but has none for Eldritch Lore, Vehicle Modification or Insight, and includes Mind Shielding. Two pairs of skills sound alike: Alchemy and Advanced Alchemy, and Explosive Disposal and Explosives.
*Possible answer:* add Mind Shielding to the list and use the older 2.1 attributes for the three missing skills (Eldritch Lore: Intelligence, Vehicle Modification: Intelligence, Insight: Wisdom). Keep the paired skills separate.
